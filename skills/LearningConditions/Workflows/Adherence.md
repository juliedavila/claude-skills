# Adherence — make the practice survive week two

**Gate:** the practice is sized to the worst week in the next month, has a named minimum version
that still counts, and has one external signal that will show abandonment before a month of it
has passed.

## Why this workflow exists

This is the hole in the material this skill was built from. Its author read out a study
concluding that motivation, not knowledge, is the real barrier to adopting better learning
methods. He agreed with it on camera. Then across ten further videos he never addressed it,
while continuing to publish timelines of months to years for the changes he recommends.

So the destination is specified in unusual detail and the trip is not specified at all. Every
technique is worthless if it is abandoned in week two, which by the source's own arithmetic is
exactly when the accumulated backlog of owed review comes due.

## Steps

### 1. Find the real shape of the week, not the imagined one

Do not ask what a normal week looks like. Ask for the *worst* week in the next four, by name and
cause. Check the actual calendar rather than accepting a description; travel, caregiving,
busy seasons, deadlines and family obligations are the true shape of the month and are
routinely omitted from self-reports of available time.

The practice gets sized to that worst week. A practice sized to a clear week is a practice that
ends the first time the week is not clear, and it ends with the conclusion "I failed at this"
rather than the accurate "I designed it wrong."

### 2. Define the minimum version that still counts

Every practice needs a floor small enough to survive a bad day, stated in advance and explicitly
counted as success rather than as a partial failure. Ten minutes of cold reconstruction counts.
One comparison between two ideas counts.

The point is not the ten minutes. It is that the streak does not break, because the story
"I stopped doing this" is what actually ends practices, and that story starts with one missed
day being scored as a failure.

### 3. Attach it to something that already survives

New practices that depend on new time usually die. Attach it to an event that already reliably
happens: an existing commute, a standing block, the hour after a recurring obligation. The
anchor should be something that has survived the last three bad weeks on its own.

Implementation intentions, meaning a specific if-this-then-that plan naming time and place, are
one of the better-replicated findings in this whole area, holding at a small-to-moderate effect
that does not erode at follow-up. This is worth more than motivation work, and notably it
survived the replication crisis that took down several constructs the source material treats as
settled. See `References/EvidenceBase.md`.

### 4. Name the backlog rule before there is a backlog

Decide in advance what happens to missed review. The default should be **forgive, do not
accumulate**. A system that carries debt forward converts one bad week into a visibly impossible
Monday, and an impossible Monday ends the practice.

This is the failure the source names well and then designs no defence against: each item you
defer is a claim on your future self, and those claims compound quietly until the total is
obviously unpayable.

### 5. Set the abandonment tripwire

Pick one external signal that abandonment has started, and say what it is now, while the
practice still feels good. Examples: two consecutive weeks with no entry in the practice log, or
a month with no cold reconstruction attempted.

The tripwire must be external, because abandonment does not feel like a decision. It feels like
a busy week, then another one. Without a signal outside your own sense of how things are going,
the first accurate report arrives months late.

## Output

The worst-week sizing, the named minimum, the anchor event, the backlog rule, and the tripwire
with its check date.

## Gotchas

- **Do not treat motivation as the lever.** The evidence for intervening on beliefs and mindset
  to change behaviour is far weaker than its popularity suggests, and effect sizes shrink as
  study quality rises. Design and scheduling are the stronger levers. `References/EvidenceBase.md`
  has the numbers.
- **Never size a practice from an aspirational week.** If the person describes their week in the
  conditional ("normally I would have"), that is the imagined week. Ask what actually happened
  last month.
- **A minimum version is not a lower standard.** It preserves the streak so that the full version
  has something to return to. Say this explicitly, because it otherwise reads as permission to
  slack and gets refused by exactly the conscientious people who need it most.
- **Do not add a second practice while the first is unstable.** Adherence is the scarce resource
  in this skill, and splitting it is the reliable way to lose both.
