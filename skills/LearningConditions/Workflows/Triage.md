# Triage — decide what this material actually deserves

**Gate:** every piece of incoming material has one named destination and, where the destination
is not "conviction," an explicit reason it does not need to be.

Most material does not deserve deep work. Treating everything as conviction-worthy is not
diligence, it is the leading cause of abandoning the practice entirely, because the implied
workload is impossible and the mind quietly notices before you do.

## Why this runs before anything else

The source corpus this skill was built from gets one thing importantly right: different *kinds*
of material need different processes, and applying one process to all of them is the most common
structural error in how people study. Its author sorts input into five kinds and routes each
differently, and that triage is what makes his otherwise-contrarian position on flashcards
coherent. The map is in `References/TriageMap.md`.

The part he does not say, and which matters more for a working adult: **one of the destinations
has to be "no."**

## Steps

### 1. Establish what the material is for

Ask, and do not skip this even when it seems obvious:

- What decision or capability does this change if you absorb it?
- If nothing changes, what is the actual motive? Curiosity and enjoyment are legitimate answers,
  and they route to skim-and-drop rather than to conviction.
- Is there a deadline or event that fixes the required depth? A talk you are giving needs a
  different depth than a field you are idly tracking.

### 2. Route to one of five destinations

Load `References/TriageMap.md` for the full test on each. In brief:

| Destination | When | What happens next |
|---|---|---|
| **Conviction** | You will have to defend, apply or teach it, and being wrong is costly | Hand off to `Internalize`. Stop here. |
| **Working reference** | You need to *find* it reliably, not hold it | Knowledge Archive ingest, indexed for retrieval |
| **Arbitrary detail** | No conceptual payload, must be recallable cold | Flashcard, and only this category earns one |
| **Skim and drop** | Worth knowing it exists, not worth holding | One note on the claim and where it lives |
| **No** | Fails every test above | Say so, and say why, out loud |

### 3. Sanity-check the routing against capacity

Count what is already in flight. If conviction-level items outnumber what the next four weeks
can carry, the triage is wrong regardless of how deserving each item felt. Downgrade the weakest
until the load fits the *worst* week in the window, not the best one.

This step is where triage actually earns its place. The failure it prevents is not choosing the
wrong destination for one item. It is choosing the right destination for twelve items and
silently taking on a workload that ends the practice.

### 4. Record the decision, including the refusals

A refusal that is not written down gets re-litigated every time the material resurfaces. One
line per decision, with the reason. The refusals are the valuable half, because they are the
ones that will otherwise keep asking for attention.

## Output

A short routing table, the reason for every non-conviction call, and an explicit load check
against the coming four weeks.

## Gotchas

- **Do not route by how interesting it is.** Interest predicts the desire to start, not the
  value of finishing. Interesting-but-no is a real and common answer.
- **Do not put conceptual material on flashcards.** This is the single most common misroute and
  the one the source material is genuinely right about. A card can test whether you can produce
  a cue's memorised partner; it cannot test whether you understand a relationship. Evidence for
  this is unusually strong, stronger than the source's own argument for it. See
  `References/EvidenceBase.md`.
- **A thing can be re-triaged later.** Skim-and-drop now does not forbid conviction in six
  months if it turns out to matter. Saying that out loud makes the refusal easier to accept.
- **Watch for conviction-routing as avoidance.** Deciding something needs deep study is an
  excellent way to never start it. If an item has been routed to conviction twice without work
  beginning, that is data about the real priority, and the honest move is to downgrade it.
