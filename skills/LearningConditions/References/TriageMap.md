# TriageMap — the five destinations and how to tell them apart

Loaded by `Workflows/Triage.md`. Route each piece of incoming material to exactly one
destination. When two seem to fit, take the cheaper one; material can always be re-triaged upward
later, and over-routing is the failure that ends practices.

## 1. Conviction

**Test:** you will have to defend it, apply it under pressure, or teach it, and being wrong is
costly.

The bar is deliberately high. Conviction work runs in months, not evenings, and every item routed
here competes with every other. If you cannot name the situation in which you will be held to it,
it is not this.

**Next step:** hand off to `Internalize`, which owns the per-topic loop, the cold-recall gate and
the later spaced review. Stop here. Do not design study technique in this skill.

## 2. Working reference

**Test:** you need to *find* it reliably, not hold it in your head. Looking it up at the moment of
use is acceptable and carries no real cost.

Most technical documentation, most standards, most numbers, and nearly everything you would
reasonably re-derive or re-read rather than recall.

**Next step:** Knowledge Archive ingest, indexed so future-you can actually retrieve it. The work
here is the indexing, not the reading.

**Common error:** routing something to conviction because forgetting it feels sloppy. Forgetting
retrievable things is correct behaviour, not a failure of discipline.

## 3. Arbitrary detail

**Test:** it has no conceptual payload, it cannot be derived from anything you know, and you must
be able to produce it cold without a lookup.

Vocabulary in a new language, drug dosages, key bindings, a small set of constants. This category
is smaller than people expect.

**Next step:** a flashcard. **This is the only destination that earns one.**

**Why the restriction is strict:** a card tests whether you can produce a cue's memorised partner.
It cannot test whether you understand a relationship, and the transfer evidence is unforgiving:
across 192 effect sizes, transfer was weakest of all to rearranged stimulus-response items, often
showing none at all. Put a concept on a card and you will learn the card. The tell is fast, fluent
recall that collapses the moment the question is reworded.

**Load rule:** if this category is generating more than a modest number of new cards a week, the
problem is upstream. Material is being misrouted here that belongs in conviction or reference.
Fix the triage rather than grinding the deck.

## 4. Skim and drop

**Test:** worth knowing that it exists and roughly what it claims, not worth holding.

Most papers, most talks, most books outside your working areas. This is the correct destination
for the large majority of interesting material, and routing honestly here is what makes the
conviction slots affordable.

**Next step:** one line on what it claims and where it lives, then let it go. If it matters, it
will resurface, and it can be re-triaged then with better information than you have now.

## 5. No

**Test:** it fails every test above, and the honest reason you are still holding it is that
dropping it feels like a loss.

**Next step:** say no out loud and write down why. An unrecorded refusal gets re-litigated every
time the material resurfaces, which is its own steady tax on attention.

This destination is not a failure state. A triage system that cannot decline anything will
eventually route everything to conviction, produce an impossible workload, and end the practice
it was meant to protect. The refusals are what make the rest affordable.

## Load check

After routing a batch, count the conviction items against the next four weeks, sized to the
**worst** week in that window rather than the best. If the load does not fit, downgrade the
weakest conviction items until it does.

Getting the destination right for each item individually, while collectively taking on more than
the calendar can carry, is the most common way this whole approach fails. It fails silently,
because each individual decision was defensible.
