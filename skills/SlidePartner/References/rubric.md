# Review rubric

This checklist is fixed on purpose. A reviewer who invents criteria while reading produces a different review each time, and audiences who sense that a deck is off usually cannot say why. Work through every item in order and give each one of four labels:

- **PASS**: the deck meets the check.
- **FLAG**: it does not. Cite the slide and say what the slide says or shows.
- **N/A**: the check does not apply to this deck.
- **NOT SEEN**: the check applies and could not be judged from what was provided, for example slide bodies when only titles were shared.

Work top-down. A deck with the wrong argument is not improved by better slides, so fit and spine come before sequence, and sequence before slides. Slide count and length are never findings by themselves.

Each item says what counts as a FLAG and what does not, because fifteen weak findings bury the three real ones. One defect often trips several checks: an ask that arrives last can flag S2, Q7 and B1 together. After the pass, fold those into one finding that lists its check IDs.

Review against the medium and purpose the author stated. If they were not stated, infer them, say so at the top, and review against the inference.

## Fit

**F1. Medium.** Is the deck rendered for the way it will be met? FLAG a deck that will be read alone and holds only titles, pictures and fragments. FLAG a deck that will be presented and holds paragraphs. FLAG one file doing both jobs when the audience for each is real. Do not flag density in a small-room discussion deck or a pre-read.

**F2. Purpose.** Can a cold audience tell whether they are being informed or asked to decide? FLAG a buy-in deck whose ask never appears as an action. FLAG a deck that mixes a full debrief and a full pitch with no seam between them. FLAG a debrief only when its content plainly calls for a choice and the deck leaves that choice unnamed. A debrief that informs and asks for nothing passes.

**F3. Deck or document.** Does the content suit slides? FLAG a long chain of reasoning broken into fragment bullets, where the links between the ideas are the point. Suggest a memo, or full-sentence titles with the relations written out. N/A when the format is required.

## Spine

**S1. The one sentence.** Can a cold audience state the deck's point in a sentence with a position and the stakes, and does it match the author's? FLAG when the restatement differs from the intake or cannot be made. This is usually the top finding. When the author stated no point, judge only whether a point can be restated, and ask for theirs.

**S2. Frame first.** Within the first slide or two, does the audience know what the deck is about and what it asks of them? FLAG an opening of agenda, team and background before any point. A signalled build passes when it opens with what is being decided and that a recommendation is coming. A live talk built on the gap passes when its first minute names the change it argues for.

**S3. Tension.** Is there a complication the audience recognizes? FLAG a deck that describes a situation and argues nothing. N/A for a recurring status readout.

**S4. Starting point.** Would this audience accept the opening situation? FLAG an opening the audience would dispute, or one that starts from the author's concerns where the audience has different ones.

## Sequence

**Q1. Titles are claims.** FLAG topic titles ("Q3 results", "Architecture", "Next steps") on slides that are meant to establish something. Quote the title and offer the claim it should make, as a frame with a query when the facts are missing. Do not flag the title slide, dividers, discussion slides that pose a question, an agenda or backup tables.

**Q2. The titles test.** Read only the titles, in order. Do they give the argument? FLAG gaps where a step is missing, repeats, and places where the order follows the author's work and not the audience's need (method before finding, history before point).

**Q3. One step per slide.** FLAG a slide whose title joins two separate claims and whose body tries to prove both, and two slides that make the same claim. Do not flag a title whose "and" joins two measures of one claim, or a dense page in a read-alone deck that still makes one claim.

**Q4. Each step earns the next.** FLAG a slide that does not follow from the one before or set up the one after, and a section that opens with no question or claim.

**Q5. Position.** For a long or complex deck, can the audience tell where they are? FLAG an agenda that is only a list of section names when the material needs a map, and a complex system explained with no view of the whole. N/A for a deck of about ten slides or fewer with one line of argument.

**Q6. Main line and appendix.** FLAG a main line carrying slides the argument holds without, and a deciding piece of evidence left in the backup. For read-alone decks, FLAG backup that is not linked from the page it supports.

**Q7. Survives a cut.** Would the first slide alone, and the first three, each give a complete message? FLAG a deck whose point arrives only at the end, unless it is a deliberate signalled build or a live talk built on the gap.

## Debrief

Apply to decks whose purpose is understanding. Otherwise N/A.

**D1. Findings up front.** FLAG findings that first appear after method and data.

**D2. Fact, judgment, confidence.** Can the audience tell what was measured from what was judged, and how sure the authors are? FLAG judgments presented as facts, bare probability words with no number, and uncertainty carried only by vague hedges.

**D3. Terms and parts before mechanism.** For an audience new to the subject, FLAG a term or component used before it is introduced. N/A for an expert audience, where FLAG the reverse: scaffolding they do not need.

**D4. Layering.** FLAG a diagram with so many elements that a newcomer could not take it in at once, shown whole with no build and no run of pages. Do not flag a diagram that reads clearly as one picture. The evidence on builds is split, so phrase this as a suggestion.

**D5. Cause.** For an account of what happened, FLAG a timeline of events with no stated links between them, and a person named as the cause.

**D6. The limits.** FLAG a debrief with no statement of what is unknown or what would change the conclusion.

## Buy-in

Apply to decks that ask for a decision. Otherwise N/A.

**B1. The ask is an action.** Who does what, by when, at what cost. FLAG "seeking alignment" or "for discussion" on a deck that needs a decision, and an ask that appears only on the last slide of an answer-first deck.

**B2. Why now, and whose goal.** FLAG a proposal with no reason for the timing, or one justified only in the author's terms when the decision maker keeps score on something else.

**B3. Strongest reasons.** FLAG a weak reason sitting beside strong ones in the main line. A count above three is a cue to look and is never the finding: five strong reasons pass.

**B4. Case and base rate.** FLAG a story with no number behind it. FLAG a deciding number with no concrete case when the decision will be made after the meeting. Where the author has no base rate, the check passes if the slide says the case is one case.

**B5. Options.** FLAG a single option shown alone, a missing "do nothing" with its cost, options compared on different criteria, and an option included only to make another look good.

**B6. Objection answered.** FLAG a deck that ignores the objection this audience is most likely to hold. FLAG, as worse, an objection or risk listed with no answer.

**B7. Weakness and risk stated.** FLAG a proposal with no stated downside, a serious risk buried in a list of minor ones, and a risk with no size or remedy. A likelihood the author cannot source passes when the slide says it is their estimate and what it rests on.

**B8. Timeline and cost grounded.** FLAG a single confident date with no range and no reference to comparable work. Where nothing comparable exists, the check passes if the slide says so and gives the basis for the range.

**B9. First sight.** If the meeting itself is the first time the deciders meet the idea, FLAG that as a risk to the decision and suggest conversations or a pre-read beforehand. A deck that is itself a pre-read sent ahead is the remedy and passes. Label this an inference when the intake does not say.

**B10. Checkable.** Apply the six checks under "Honest persuasion" in `SKILL.md`. FLAG any that fail, and rank them above every other buy-in finding. A stated unknown passes checks 2 and 5.

## Rendering for a live deck

Apply to decks that will be presented. Otherwise N/A.

**L1. Voice has room.** FLAG slides that hold the script, most of all beside a figure. FLAG a point that lives only in the speaker's words while a dense slide competes with it.

**L2. What must be held is visible.** FLAG a comparison that depends on a number from an earlier slide, and a decision asked for with the options no longer on screen.

**L3. Builds.** FLAG bullet lists animated one at a time as a habit. For a diagram, apply D4. The evidence is split, so these are suggestions.

**L4. Signposts.** FLAG section changes with nothing to mark them, on the slide or in the notes.

## Rendering for a read-alone deck

Apply to pre-reads, leave-behinds and forwarded decks. Otherwise N/A.

**R1. Stands alone.** Walk the eight items in `medium.md`. FLAG each one missing: the answer on the first page (and the ask, when there is one), reasoning between slides, definitions, how to read each chart, position, sources, predictable questions, what happens next.

**R2. Summary page.** FLAG a pre-read with no page a reader could stop after. In a reading deck the opening page is that page.

**R3. Not in the notes.** FLAG explanation that exists only in speaker notes.

**R4. Reading time.** FLAG a pre-read whose length is out of proportion to the time its readers have. State the estimate.

## Slide

Apply to every content slide. Report the worst three or four cases of each, not every instance.

**X1. Body supports title.** FLAG a body that supports a different claim, and a body that supports none.

**X2. Structure shown as structure.** FLAG bullets that hide a relationship the claim depends on: steps whose order is the point, options that need comparing on shared criteria, a hierarchy, a timeline. Do not flag a plain list of parallel items. Hand the design to `VisualizeReview`.

**X3. One thing marked.** FLAG a chart or diagram where nothing shows the audience what the title is about.

**X4. Words by the thing.** FLAG legends and facing bullet columns where direct labels would do.

**X5. Caveat placement.** FLAG a caveat that would change the decision sitting in a sub-bullet, a footnote or small type.

**X6. Numbers in context.** FLAG a number with no unit, period, comparison or source.

**X7. Beside the point.** FLAG decorative images, asides and extra facts that serve no claim. Do not flag a single case that carries the argument.

**X8. Changes mean something.** FLAG the same entity in different colours or shapes across slides, and layout changes that signal nothing. N/A to whatever a fixed template dictates.

**X9. Lifts out.** FLAG slides that make no sense alone: "as shown earlier", an unlabelled axis, no date on a status figure.

## What this rubric does not cover

Say so in the note under "Not reviewed": whether the numbers and facts are right, chart and diagram design (`VisualizeReview`), sentence-level wording (`WritingPartner`), the merit of the proposal itself, and delivery.
